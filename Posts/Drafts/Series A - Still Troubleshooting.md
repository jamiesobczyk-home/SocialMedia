For most of my career, my pride came from the CLI. Give me a switch, a router, and a problem nobody else could crack, and I was happy. I knew I was good at it.

Then I moved into management and the problems stopped being technical. I figured I'd just miss the old work. I did, for a while.

What took me longer to understand is that the thing I loved was never the CLI. It was the puzzle. Finding what nobody else could find. That part came with me.

A people problem has a root cause. So does a project that keeps slipping. You still gather information, form a theory, test it, and pay attention when the evidence doesn't fit what you expected. It took me a while to admit it, but I get the same thrill out of solving one of those that I used to get from chasing down a routing loop at two in the morning.

I didn't have to give up the technical side either. I get to use it every time I talk through a problem with one of my engineers. Asking questions. Making suggestions. Letting them drive.

Some of them are better at this than I ever was. I get to watch that happen, and occasionally have a hand in why.

I miss the keyboard. I wouldn't trade what replaced it.
