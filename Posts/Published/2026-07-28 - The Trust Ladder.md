At home, I've been leaning on AI a lot. It plans meals for the family. It's helping me build an inventory of every tool in the shop. It even recommends what I should read next, based on a few lists I've fed it. In every case, it's making a suggestion. I'm the one who decides what actually happens.

At work, it's a different story. Every piece of software I touch is pitching built-in AI that goes further than suggestions, all the way through to implementing changes on my infrastructure. And I keep thinking about how long it takes the people on my team to earn that kind of access. Nobody gets write permissions to production on day one. You earn that rung by rung, by proving you understand what you're touching and what happens when you're wrong.

It's already a leap of faith trusting the humans I work with to make changes carefully. I don't know yet what it takes to trust an agent the same way.

I know the day is coming when AI agents on the network are just background noise, the same way automated patching or scripted failovers are now. I'm just not there yet, and I'd rather be late to that trust than early to a bad night.

Where does the AI in your environment actually sit? Suggesting things to you, or already touching production?
